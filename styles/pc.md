## Reviewer's summary

Checked against Pouet and Demozoo page by page (about 60 Pouet production pages read for credits and comments, their screenshots viewed as contact sheets, and about 45 Demozoo entries fetched as a second source), plus Wikipedia, Lode Vandevenne's effect tutorials and one blog write-up. Defacto2 is still unreachable (HTTP 403 to fetch, a bot-verification page in the browser, which I did not try to pass), and the web-search allowance for this session was already used up, so nothing here rests on search results.

The main finding stands: there is no single "Razor 1911 look" on PC. Going through Razor's Pouet list (305 entries, 189 of them DOS/Windows/Linux, not the "600+" the research claimed) the screenshots fall into these phases: (1) 1990-93 DOS loaders: a red capsule logo on blue gradient bars with a huge gradient scroller (Silent Shadow, with Skillion), a flaming, dripping logo over a starfield (1992 loader, Maelstrom 1993), a bronze extruded serif logo (Battle Chess 4000, 1993); (2) 1993-94 trainer menus; (3) the 1996-98 numbered DOS cracktros by Hetero of SAC, almost all PC conversions of Amiga intros: flat colour field, pixel logo, centred presents-text, one effect; (4) 1998-2000 Win9x screens with painted fantasy art and a fill-in template; (5) 2001-03 one-idea cracktros with Dubmood music; (6) the 2006 comeback cartoon and 2007 keygen templates with the same mascot; (7) 2010-12 rez-coded rainbow cube and glenz work; (8) the 2016 chrome-spheres cracktro by XT95; (9) 2024-26: text-mode installers drawn by Goto80, desktop-shattering "D-cracktros", the BIOS-screen dentro CMOS Cosmos (1st, Compusphere 2025) and the anniversary demo Razor1911 (1st, Revision 2026). The logo changes nearly every time; the rainbow block logo many people think of as "the" Razor logo is, according to Pouet comments by ACiD's radman and by rez, an ACiD text-art logo by JED from 1996.

Around Razor sit the broader schools: early-90s DOS VGA loaders (THG, INC, USA/Fairlight, TRSI), mid-90s Mode-X conversions of Amiga intros (SAC for Razor, Hybrid, Prestige), Win9x rip installers and trainers (Class, Myth, Hoodlum, Deviance), and the 2004-11 Win32 "new old-school" revival (Hoodlum, Deviance, Reloaded, Skid Row, Genesis with Titan/TRSI coders and artists).

I return 14 styles: the 13 submitted were kept in substance, with "Lit and liquid" merged into two neighbours, the recent Razor text-mode installers split out from the skinned installers because they can be done as pure text, and one new Razor style added (BIOS setup hijack). If a shorter list is wanted, the ones most distinct from the existing six and best sourced are: chrome spheres over checkerboard, neon cube field, text-mode poster installer, BIOS setup hijack, pencil on paper, fake desktop takeover, trainer menu and the Razor flat-field layout. Keygen windows by TSRh, AiR and CORE were named in the brief but could not be researched (no search budget, Defacto2 blocked); the existing keygen-dialog style covers that object, and the only keygen material confirmed here is Razor's 2007 templates (a dancing egg-headed mascot above a gold GENERATE KEY plate; graphics GB, code blkpanther, music Dubmood).

Motion is inferred from comments and effect names, not watched: every visual description comes from a single Pouet screenshot per production. SVG techniques marked "tested" were tested by the original researcher in Chromium only; I did not re-run them.

## pc-01 · Razor 1911 DOS cracktro: flat field, pixel logo, presents-text

**Animated SVG** · build: **Easy** · impact: **3/5** · 1996-1998, MS-DOS (320x240x8 Mode-X, GUS/SoundBlaster per Demozoo); Razor 1911's numbered cracktro series made with Superior Art Creations, code by Hetero

The small numbered intros that ran in front of Razor's mid-90s game releases: one flat colour field, a pixel logo across the top, a few centred lines of blocky text, one background effect and a looping tracker tune. Almost all are PC conversions of older Amiga intros (Pouet commenters name Paso for \#7, Aspect for \#01, Supplex for Arnie 3, Skid Row for \#06, and Demozoo tags \#10 melon-dezign-inspired), which is exactly why they feel 'Amiga' on a PC. The release titles shown in them are filler: Demozoo lists blank template versions.

**What it looks like**

- One saturated flat background per intro: deep aubergine (\#09), pale grey-green (\#10), lilac (\#7), navy (\#5)
- A different hand-pixelled logo each time, pinned to the top edge: tan bevelled italic capitals with pointed serifs (\#09, logo by Poise per Demozoo), a multicolour script wordmark with a flower (\#7, logo by Dream Design), a green outline logo on navy (\#5)
- A small wooden-looking plaque or bar directly under the logo carrying the release title (\#09)
- A centred column of wide, letterspaced capitals: group name between two hearts, stars or arrows, a dashed rule, a 'proudly presents on &lt;date&gt;' line, then the title between asterisks
- Exactly one background effect: large pale cogs meshing slowly behind the text (\#10), a lilac-and-white checker floor rotating in perspective with distance fog (\#7), or nothing but a fading text writer (\#5)
- Text is white with a 1px dark outline so it reads over the effect; \#5 sets red and magenta text and magenta dashed rules on navy instead
- \#01 variant: the frame split diagonally into a black half and a grey dithered half with a huge lowercase wordmark across the split, waving
- Chunky 320x240 pixels, no anti-aliasing

**Palette.** One field colour, white text, a two- or three-tone logo. Aubergine about \#2a0a3a with tan/gold logo; grey-green about \#b9c9c0 with lighter cogs; lilac about \#b080e0 with white/lavender checker; navy with green logo, red text and magenta rules. Hex values are read off screenshots, not sampled from the originals.

**Lettering.** 8x8 or 16x16 bitmap capitals, square and wide, usually spaced one cell apart; hearts, stars and arrows as bullets; dashed rules made of hyphens. \#5 uses the Amiga Topaz face according to a Pouet comment. Logos are drawn wordmarks, not fonts.

**Motion.** Text lines fade or type in one after another, hold, then swap to the next page (credits, greetings). The background loops: cogs turn, the checker floor spins. A Pouet comment on \#09 lists a zoomed logo and scrolling text, so some of them do scroll.

**As a README header.** One SVG banner about 800x300. Flat colour fill; the project wordmark drawn as pixel rects at the top; a small plaque with the version; then three or four centred lines: project name letterspaced between two glyphs, a dashed rule, a presents-line with the release date, the tagline. One looping background effect behind the text (cogs are the cheap choice). Install command and links go in normal Markdown directly below.

**How to build it.** Pure shapes and CSS. Cogs: two or three gear paths with a rotate keyframe, opposite directions, fill a few percent lighter than the field. Text pages: groups with opacity keyframes and staggered delays. Pixel font: emit each glyph as rects or one path (no web fonts, and do not rely on a system monospace for the blocky look). A few KB, no filters. Only the rotating checker-floor variant is harder; use the chrome-spheres style for that.

**Do not copy / caveats.** Do not copy any Razor wordmark, the 1911 name or the slogans. The compositions are conversions of Amiga intros, so treat cogs-behind-text and checker-floor-under-text as genre conventions and do not trace either. The game titles in these screens are template filler, not releases. Demozoo credits Hetero with code on \#02, \#03, \#09, \#10 and \#11 but only with the font on \#7, so 'coded by Hetero' is not confirmed for every number. Pale text on a pale field fails contrast without the dark outline.

## pc-02 · Plasma field with the logo embossed in it

**Animated SVG** · build: **Medium** · impact: **4/5** · 1996-1997 on MS-DOS (Razor Crack Intro \#02 and \#03, code and graphics by Hetero); plasma as a backdrop survives into Windows cracktros (Hoodlum's LUCiD 4000th, Feb 2005). The effect itself is an early-90s demo staple.

A full-screen single-hue plasma in which the group's wordmark appears as part of the plasma, as if pressed into it. Razor's two versions are the ones Pouet commenters single out, mostly for the tune and the little cartoon sprite. Demozoo notes that \#02 picks its colour scheme at random on start, so the crimson and green seen in screenshots are two rolls of the same intro design.

**What it looks like**

- The whole frame filled with soft, slowly crawling bands of one hue: crimson/magenta in the \#02 screenshot, green in \#03
- Tall heavy serif capitals of the wordmark visible only as a lighter/darker displacement inside the plasma, tilted back in perspective so the letters lean away
- Thin black letterbox bars top and bottom
- A tiny cartoon sprite and a line of small white text in the top-left corner
- Nothing else on screen: no frame, no starfield; text appears over the plasma in pages
- Later descendant: plasma plus scroller plus chiptune as the default cracktro recipe (a commenter's dismissive summary of LUCiD 4000th)

**Palette.** Monochrome ramp from near-black through one hue to a pale tint: green about \#001a00 to \#7dff5a, crimson about \#1a0010 to \#ff5aa0. Pick the hue at random per build to echo the original's random palette.

**Lettering.** Roman-style heavy serif capitals for the wordmark, rendered by the effect rather than drawn as a bitmap. Body text in a small white bitmap font.

**Motion.** Palette cycling: the field is computed once and the colour index shifts each frame, so the bands crawl while the shapes barely move; a slow sine drift bends the field (mechanism per Lode Vandevenne's tutorial, not observed in the cracktro itself).

**As a README header.** A wide, short SVG strip (about 800x200) that is nothing but plasma with the project name embossed in it, letterboxed top and bottom. Tagline and install line sit underneath as ordinary Markdown. Works well as a thin strip above a text-only NFO block.

**How to build it.** Filter route (researcher tested in a Chromium &lt;img&gt;, not re-tested): feTurbulence fractalNoise at low baseFrequency (about 0.012, 2 octaves) into feColorMatrix type=hueRotate with a SMIL animate on values 0 to 360, then feComponentTransfer with discrete tables to band it into one hue. This gives palette-cycling without script, but it is cloud noise rather than true sine plasma and the filter re-rasterises every frame, so keep the strip small. Logo: clip a second copy of the plasma with a phase offset inside the letter shapes, or use the letters as the map for feDisplacementMap. No-filter fallback that is cheaper and closer to sine plasma: four or five large radialGradient ellipses drifting on CSS keyframes with mix-blend-mode screen, with gradient stop colours keyframed. Safari and Firefox behaviour of the animated filter is unverified.

**Do not copy / caveats.** Do not reuse Razor's wordmark or the corner sprites (commenters identify them as existing cartoon characters). Plasma is generic. Corrections to the submitted research: the Paradox 2000 cracktro cited as 'green plasma' is described by Pouet commenters as a blurred tunnel and has been moved to the tunnel style; the fire effect was removed from this style because no named PC cracktro using it was confirmed. Music for \#03 is contested (Demozoo: Spoon; a Pouet commenter: Croaker; Demozoo says versions with different music exist), so it is not credited here. Full-frame colour cycling can be uncomfortable: cycle in 6 seconds or more and freeze under prefers-reduced-motion.

- [Same on Pouet: green plasma screenshot; comment 'electronic plasma logotype in the background'](<https://www.pouet.net/prod.php?which=642>)
- [Razor Crack Intro \#02 on Pouet, Dec 1996: crimson plasma screenshot; comments on plasma background, sprite and tune](<https://www.pouet.net/prod.php?which=643>)

## pc-03 · Chrome spheres over a checkerboard

**Animated SVG** · build: **Medium** · impact: **5/5** · Razor Cracktro \#7 (DOS, Jan 1997) for the spinning checker floor; Hoodlum cracktro \#1 (Windows, Nov 2004) and Skid Row's Streets of Moscow (May 2009) for checker floors under logos; Razor 1911's Deus Ex: Mankind Divided cracktro (Windows/Linux, 21 Nov 2016, code XT95) as the definitive version

Mirror balls bouncing above an endless checker floor under a chrome logo: the scene's own in-joke for 'a demo'. Razor's 2016 cracktro plays it straight; rez (who says in the comment that he was not involved, though Pouet credits him with the font) invites everyone to post their 'no chrome spheres over checkerboard' meme, and the binary quoted in the comments is literally named after the checker. It reads as retro 3D instantly, even to people who never saw a cracktro.

**What it looks like**

- Checkerboard floor in light and dark grey receding to a low horizon, squares foreshortened with distance
- A loose row of five or six blue-tinted mirror spheres hovering just above the floor, each reflecting floor and sky, each with a soft shadow
- A large italic chrome logo floating in the sky: steel-blue gradient faces, a lighter bevel, sharp diagonal cuts, slight perspective tilt
- Sky as a smooth gradient that changes through the loop (Demozoo tag day-night-cycle; commenters praise the sun rising and setting); dusty mauve-grey in the Pouet screenshot
- A scroller running small in the distance at floor level
- Earlier cousins: lilac-and-white spinning checker with fog in Razor \#7; bright blue-and-black checker under a bending blue logo with a fat yellow 3D scroller in Skid Row 2009; a spinning chessboard with balls morphing out of it in Hoodlum cracktro \#1

**Palette.** Floor about \#e8e8e8 and \#4a4a4a; sky from a dusty pink or mauve at the horizon to blue above; chrome logo in steel blues (about \#1b3a66, \#5d8fd0, white highlight); spheres as blue-tinted mirrors. Night variant: same layout under a dark indigo sky.

**Lettering.** Logo: wide italic capitals with diagonal cuts and a horizontal chrome break line (logo by Sprocket per both databases). Scroller: a bespoke bitmap font (credited separately to rez on Pouet).

**Motion.** Spheres bounce in a phase-shifted wave; the floor moves; the sky cycles slowly from day to night; the scroller crosses at the horizon; reflections follow. Commenters found the scroller slow.

**As a README header.** A 16:9-ish SVG hero (about 800x340): sky gradient, checker floor, a row of chrome spheres (one per feature, or one per letter of a short name), the project wordmark as the chrome logo in the sky, a one-line scroller at the horizon. Plain Markdown below for the install line and links.

**How to build it.** SVG has no perspective transform, so the generator projects the floor: emit it as 12-16 precomputed trapezoid rows of alternating quads. Sideways scroll is the cheap motion: each row group gets the same translateX keyframe with a per-row distance (inline CSS variable), clipped to the frame. Forward motion needs each row boundary animated to the next boundary's position with a colour swap at the wrap (SMIL animate on points), which is fiddlier. Spheres: radialGradient plus a clipped, squashed copy of checker and sky as the reflection, translateY keyframes with staggered delays; shadows are blurred ellipses scaling inversely. Chrome logo: linearGradient with a hard stop at the horizon line plus a light stroke; glyphs as paths. Day-night: keyframe the gradient stop colours. Well under 50 KB.

**Do not copy / caveats.** Do not copy Razor's chrome wordmark or Sprocket's letterforms. Spheres on a checker floor is a shared scene cliche and free to use. The coder discrepancy in the submitted research is resolved: Pouet's 'Anat' and Demozoo's 'XT95' are the same person (rez's comment credits XT95, the quoted binary name contains XT95, and Demozoo lists XT95 as a member). Moving floors can cause motion discomfort: keep it slow and honour prefers-reduced-motion.

- [Same on Demozoo: 21 Nov 2016, code XT95, music Dubmood and wasp, logo Sprocket, tag day-night-cycle](<https://demozoo.org/productions/165425/>)
- [Hoodlum cracktro \#1 on Pouet (Nov 2004): fake copper-split bars, rotating chessboard, balls morphing out of the checkerboard, metaballs](<https://www.pouet.net/prod.php?which=14107>)
- [Same on Demozoo: 2 Nov 2004, code hitchhikr, graphics Alien, music Xerxes](<https://demozoo.org/productions/8425/>)

## pc-04 · Early DOS VGA loader: metal logo, gradient bars, one big scroller

**Animated SVG** · build: **Easy** · impact: **3/5** · 1990-1993, MS-DOS VGA (mode 13h, 256 colours), often silent or AdLib; The Humble Guys, INC, USA/Fairlight, TRSI with The Dream Team, and Razor 1911's first PC intros (with Skillion)

The first generation of graphical intros on the IBM PC, made before the platform had its own demoscene. Wikipedia credits The Humble Guys with bringing animated graphical intros, the NFO file and an intro-making tool to the PC. They are blunt next to Amiga work, which is the charm: a big metal logo, gradient bars, one line of oversized scrolling text. Razor's own first PC loaders (1990-93) belong here and are what the group looked like before the numbered series.

**What it looks like**

- Group initials as a heavy framed plate: chrome letters with a blue-to-white 'sky' gradient in the upper half and a brown 'ground' gradient below, inside a grey bevelled frame (THG Lemmings, 1991)
- Fat gold 3D serif capitals on black with specular highlights, and the group's full name set on a sine arc in small magenta capitals beneath (INC, 1992)
- Background of nested concentric diamonds in one hue (electric blue on black), animated by palette rotation
- Horizontal gradient bars in purple, orange and blue top and bottom, or passing through the logo as copper-style bars (INC Europe JOOP \#2; a Pouet commenter calls THG's bars pseudo-3D copper bars)
- Razor variant 1 (Silent Shadow, with Skillion): a red logo inside a rounded chrome capsule on deep-blue gradient bars, the partner's logo in a matching capsule below, and a scroller in very large red-to-orange gradient letters across the middle
- Razor variant 2 (1992 loader, Maelstrom 1993): the name in red-to-yellow flame lettering with drips hanging off the bottom of each letter, over a starfield
- Razor variant 3 (Battle Chess 4000, 1993): a huge bronze extruded serif wordmark with '1911' beneath, 'Another crack from' in small white type above, green scroller below
- Single-line scroller in an oversized chunky font along the bottom edge; two crews' logos stacked when they co-released (USA over Fairlight, 1992)

**Palette.** Smooth VGA ramps: electric blue on black; gold (about \#fff2a0 to \#8a5a00); chrome (white, sky blue, brown); purple bars (about \#ff7bff to \#4a0a6a); flame (red \#c00000 through orange to yellow). TRSI's Blues Brothers intro uses striped red-brown bars behind a gold skull with red eyes.

**Lettering.** Logos are hand-drawn block or serif capitals with heavy bevel. Scrollers use oversized 16-32px bitmap fonts with squared counters and a vertical gradient fill.

**Motion.** Mostly palette cycling: the background pattern seems to pulse outward while no pixel moves. The scroller steps sideways a few pixels at a time (commenters call TRSI's 'choppy'). In the livelier ones the logo bounces with copper bars behind it, text rotates along a spline (JOOP \#2), or the scroller rides an arch (Razor/Skillion Conan intro, 1991).

**As a README header.** SVG at about 800x250: black or deep-blue field with a palette-cycled diamond pattern or gradient bars, the project's initials as a framed metal plate (or a flame-lettered wordmark) in the centre, the full project name on an arc beneath, and one oversized gradient scroller along the bottom carrying tagline and greets. A text-only block can follow for the README body.

**How to build it.** Palette cycling maps onto keyframed fills on a set of nested polygons with staggered animation-delay, or animated gradient stops: no filters, tiny file. Plate: two or three linearGradients and inset strokes. Flame lettering: a vertical red-to-yellow gradient on glyph paths plus a few hand-placed drip shapes. Arc text: glyph paths placed along a precomputed arc (textPath works but depends on system fonts). Scroller: translateX on a long group with steps() timing to reproduce the choppy scroll. Distinct from the existing Amiga style by the static framed plate, the smooth VGA ramps and the step-scroll.

**Do not copy / caveats.** Do not reuse THG, INC, USA, Fairlight, TRSI, Skillion or Razor logos or initials. Bevelled metal lettering, flame lettering and palette-cycled bars are generic. The THG plate itself is reported to be a remake of an Amiga intro. Dates differ between databases (INC Indy 4: Dec vs 6 Nov 1992; Silent Shadow: Nov 1990 vs 1991). A concentric pattern pulsing fast is a strobing risk: slow it down and provide a reduced-motion static frame.

## pc-05 · Win32 new old-school cracktro: metal logo, twister, sine scroller

**Animated SVG** · build: **Medium** · impact: **4/5** · 2004-2011, Windows (DirectX/OpenGL, often 64k or less); Hoodlum, Deviance, Reloaded, Skid Row with Titan and Electric Druggies, Genesis with TRSI

The mid-2000s revival in which demoscene coders (hitchhikr, the Titan people, anesthetic) and pixel artists (Alien^PDX, H2o, iks/irokos, bull) built deliberately old-school cracktros for ISO groups on modern hardware. It is what most people picture for a 2000s cracktro: a big airbrushed chrome or graffiti logo, a twisting column, a bouncing scroller and a chiptune. This entry now also absorbs the 'shiny' sub-look (logo on a mirror floor, lit relief) that was submitted as a separate style.

**What it looks like**

- A large custom logo as the centrepiece: brushed-chrome 3D block letters (Reloaded), liquid-chrome script with drips (Deviance), gold wildstyle graffiti on black (Skidrow 2007), chrome-purple extruded capitals (Hoodlum Stronghold 2), grey-green metal (Genesis)
- Twisters: a vertical bar that appears to twist like a wrung towel, one each side of the logo (Shrek 2 by Genesis/TRSI, praised as sine-twisters; Deviance 'Mission completed' is praised for its twister)
- A scroller riding a sine wave or bending in 3D: pink on black (Hoodlum Stronghold 2), made of large LED-dot or cube letters along the bottom (Reloaded \#4), a twisted 3D scroller that is famously unreadable (Hoodlum \#3)

## pc-07 · Text-mode poster installer (Razor 1911, 2024-26)

**Text + SVG** · build: **Easy** · impact: **4/5** · May 2024 onward, Windows; Razor 1911 game installers with code by Black Panther, graphics by Goto80 and music by Dubmood, plus the text-art passages of the 2026 anniversary demo Razor1911

## pc-11 · BIOS setup hijack: the firmware screen that starts misbehaving

**Text + SVG** · build: **Easy** · impact: **4/5** · Razor 1911, CMOS Cosmos (Windows, 29 Nov 2025; by Anat, Dubmood, Flopine, Goto80 and zabutom), 1st in the combined demo/intro competition at Compusphere 2025. A dentro rather than a cracktro, but it is current Razor and the look is a PC-only one.

A demo staged inside a PC BIOS setup screen: the familiar navy text-mode page with a double-line frame and a key legend, whose main panel then fills with text-mode effects. Pouet commenters single out 'the old school CMOS screens' and 'the BIOS part', one wishes for a whole demo inside those UI constraints, another calls it the perfect prank for an unlocked computer. Demozoo lists it as nominated for Best Direction at the Meteoriks 2026 and tags it with an epilepsy warning.

**What it looks like**

- Full-screen navy blue text mode with a single centred title line at the top reading like a setup utility name and copyright
- A white double-line box-drawing frame divided into a large upper panel and a shallow lower area
- A vertical divider down the middle of the upper panel, as in a two-column setup menu
- Inside the upper panel, the effect: rows of horizontal bars shading from orange at top and bottom to magenta in the middle, with a diamond-shaped cluster of blue arrow glyphs pointing outward from the centre line
- A key legend in small white text in the lower area: a quit key and a save-and-exit key at the left, arrow keys and 'select item' at the right
- A one-line hint in yellow, centred in its own box at the very bottom
- Everything stays on the 80x25 character grid, even the effect

**Palette.** Navy \#0000a8 field, white frame and legend, yellow hint text, red for a selection bar (standard setup-screen convention); the effect adds orange-to-magenta bars and blue arrows.

**Lettering.** The PC 8x16 text-mode font throughout: box-drawing double lines for the frame, arrow and triangle glyphs used as effect pixels, capitals for menu items.

**Motion.** Not observed beyond one still. Comments describe a BIOS screen that lingers and then takes off, a 'dot ball' part and strong music sync; assume a static menu first, then character-cell effects inside the panel.

**As a README header.** A setup menu is already a table of contents. Text-only version: a &lt;pre&gt; box with a title line, two columns of menu items that are real links to README sections (for example FEATURES SETUP, INTEGRATED TOOLS, LOAD DEFAULTS as quick start, SAVE &amp; EXIT as license), a key legend and a one-line hint that is the tagline. SVG version: the same screen in navy, white and yellow with a red selection bar stepping through the items, after which the upper panel is taken over by a character-cell effect. Use either, or the SVG above the &lt;pre&gt;.

**How to build it.** Text route: box-drawing double lines (U+2554 family) and plain capitals in a code block, 78 columns; links inside &lt;pre&gt; work. SVG route: a navy rect, the frame as crisp 2px double strokes (or glyph paths), text as pixel-font paths or rects, a red rect stepping between rows with steps() timing, a blinking cursor. The takeover effect stays cheap because it lives on an 80x25 grid: rows of rects whose fills are keyframed with per-row delays for the colour bars, and a few dozen glyph shapes switched on and off with opacity keyframes for the diamond. 10-30 KB.

**Do not copy / caveats.** Do not copy the title line: the screenshot's heading names a real BIOS vendor and copyright notice, which must not be reproduced; write a generic 'SETUP UTILITY' line with the project's own name. Do not copy Razor's effect designs. A blue setup screen is a generic convention and free to use. Only one still was seen, so the motion description is second-hand. The original carries an epilepsy warning: keep any takeover effect slow and low-contrast and freeze it under prefers-reduced-motion. It is close in spirit to the existing phosphor-terminal style but differs in colour, layout (menu, not shell) and the takeover idea.

## pc-12 · Neon cube field: the rez-era Razor look

**Animated SVG** · build: **Medium** · impact: **4/5** · 2010-2012, Windows; Razor 1911 cracktros and 64k intros coded by rez with music by Dubmood (The Settlers 7 cracktro, 27 Apr 2010; StarCraft 2 cracktro, 31 July 2010; Insert No Coins, 2 Oct 2010; The Scene Is Dead, 8 Apr 2012). A DOS ancestor exists in Razor Crack Intro \#13.

When Razor's demo section came back around 2010 it had a consistent look: fields of glowing rainbow cubes and glenz shapes, a pixel logo, scanlines and a Dubmood chiptune. Insert No Coins won the 64k competition at Main 2010 and the public-choice award at the Scene.org Awards; The Scene Is Dead placed third in PC 64k at Revision 2012. For many people this is 'the Razor 1911 one'. The rainbow-cubes idea is older than rez's use of it: the undated DOS Crack Intro \#13 already shows a 9x7 grid of cubes, one hue per cube.

**What it looks like**

- Hundreds of small cubes in a regular grid, each a different hue, so the field reads as a continuous rainbow ramp
- The field bent into a tunnel, a rolling wave landscape, or a wall seen in perspective (the four panels of the Insert No Coins screenshot)
- Glow on everything against near-black
- A segmented, LCD-like pixel wordmark pinned to the top centre of the frame (logo by iLKke on Insert No Coins)
- Rows of small translucent glenz polyhedra ordered by hue behind a rainbow block-letter logo, under horizontal scanlines (Settlers 7 cracktro)
- Text as three-dimensional ASCII characters and a rotating 3D ASCII logo, a starfield with a cube, turning cogs and a sine scroller (StarCraft 2 cracktro, per comments)
- CRT treatment: scanlines, vignette and phosphor glow, which several commenters found overdone (The Scene Is Dead)
- DOS ancestor: flat-shaded rainbow cubes in a grid with the logo running vertically down the left edge (\#13)

**Palette.** Near-black background with a full-spectrum hue ramp at high saturation across the field. The Scene Is Dead narrows to amber, cyan and magenta on black (Vivian Voss's write-up).

**Lettering.** A pixel wordmark built from segments; body text in a small square bitmap font in white or pale cyan; extruded ASCII characters in the StarCraft 2 cracktro; text-writer delivery.

**Motion.** From comments and stills: camera travel through or over the cube field, cubes rippling in a wave, glenz objects spinning, text typing on, scanlines fixed on top. Not watched.

**As a README header.** SVG hero about 800x340 on black: an isometric field of small cubes rippling in a sine wave with the hue sweeping across it, the project wordmark as a segmented pixel logo at the top centre, one line of text typing on, scanlines over everything. Install line and links below in Markdown.

**How to build it.** A fixed isometric view is practical: emit a 16x8 grid of cubes (three polygons each: top, left and right faces at three lightness levels of the cube's hue), give every cube the same translateY keyframe with a negative delay derived from its grid position so a wave travels across, and set hues statically by column. About 400 polygons and one keyframe, 60-120 KB; reduce to 12x6 to halve it. Scanlines: a 2px pattern overlay. Vignette: a radial gradient. Glow: a single static blurred duplicate layer at most; an animated blur over 400 moving shapes is too expensive. The camera fly-through and cube tunnel of the originals would need per-frame geometry and exceed the budget: not possible as such.

**Do not copy / caveats.** Do not copy Razor's pixel wordmark (a credited artist's logo) or the name. The rainbow block logo in the Settlers 7 cracktro is, according to Pouet comments by ACiD's radman and by rez, an ACiD text-art logo by JED from 1996 reused with an altered signature, which drew an objection: a concrete example of why borrowed logos must be avoided. Cube fields, glenz and scanlines are generic. \#13's date is unconfirmed (none on Pouet; Demozoo says 1994, which sits oddly with the 1996-98 numbering). Wow lowered from 5 to 4 because the buildable version is a static-camera cube wave without bloom. Saturated rainbow motion across the full width is tiring: keep amplitude and speed low, freeze under prefers-reduced-motion, and test scrolling on a phone.

## pc-13 · Vector objects: wireframe, glenz, vector balls, dot scroller, metaballs

**Animated SVG** · build: **Medium** · impact: **4/5** · Effect vocabulary from early-90s demos; in PC cracktros 1998-2010 (Razor \#12 beta on DOS; Titan with Backlash, FANAiON, Electric Druggies/Skid Row, Class, Hoodlum and Razor on Windows)

The family of effects where one geometric object rotates in the middle of the screen while the text does the talking. Wikipedia's demo-effect article lists them under vector graphics and describes glenz as partly see-through models with a diamond-like look. Sceners are openly sentimental about it: a Pouet commenter on a 2005 Hoodlum trainer asks why contemporary crack intros have no glenz. Metaballs, submitted separately, are folded in here as the 'liquid' member of the same family.

**What it looks like**

- Wireframe: a solid drawn as thin single-colour lines, rotating faintly behind huge white block capitals zooming past on a cyan field (Razor \#12 beta), or in front of concentric blue circles zooming inward with ASCII text scrolling up (Titan and Backlash, 2005)
- Glenz: small translucent polyhedra in rows, tinted in rainbow order (Razor Settlers 7), or pink-and-purple crystals drifting over a purple checker tunnel with a ribbon logo (Electric Druggies and Skid Row, Gene Troopers)
- Vector balls and dot lettering: shaded dots arranged into shapes or into the group name (FANAiON and Titan trainer; commenters credit a dot effect and 'vectorballs')
- Dot scroller: a band of text made of points, wrapped around a rotating 3D shape that morphs (Class 'orcus' installer)
- Metaballs: three z-shaded blobs fusing and separating under a chrome logo (Hoodlum and Titan, Stronghold 2), or balls morphing up out of a checkerboard (Hoodlum cracktro \#1)
- Flat blobs: soft olive-gold discs drifting behind a khaki logo and white text-writer lines (SAC Blizzard cracktro, 1999; a commenter guesses 'fake 2d metaballs')
- Background kept plain so the object reads: flat colour, slow gradient or starfield

**Palette.** Wireframe: one line colour on a flat field (white on cyan, cyan on black). Glenz: white plus one tint at about 50 percent opacity, brighter where faces overlap. Vector balls: one or two ball colours with a white highlight. Metaballs: one saturated colour with depth shading.

**Lettering.** Not font-led: text is whatever the host cracktro uses. Razor \#12 beta uses huge white block capitals; the Backlash cracktro uses ASCII text.

**Motion.** Continuous rotation on two or three axes at different speeds; objects bounce or drift on a sine path; dot objects morph between shapes; glenz faces change tint as they turn; blobs orbit, merge and pinch apart.

**As a README header.** The centrepiece of a minimal header: one rotating wireframe or glenz solid (or a trio of metaballs) at the left or centre, project name and tagline set beside it in a pixel or mono face, on a flat field. It also drops into other styles here: a glenz object beside the trainer menu, or in the viewport of the installer.

**How to build it.** The generator does the 3D offline: rotate the vertex list for 40-60 frames and emit SMIL animate elements with a values list for each face's points (the researcher confirmed polygon-points animation in a Chromium &lt;img&gt;) or each edge's endpoints. Glenz: faces at fill-opacity 0.5 in two tints, optionally mix-blend-mode screen so overlaps brighten; no depth sorting needed. Wireframe: lines only, a few KB. Vector balls and dots: circles with animated cx, cy and r; SVG cannot re-sort by depth mid-loop, so keep dots identical and additive, or choose a motion where order never flips. Metaballs: circles moved by CSS keyframes inside a group filtered with feGaussianBlur then an feColorMatrix alpha threshold (the 'gooey' trick, tested by the researcher); the filter re-renders every frame, so keep its region small. CSS rotateY on SVG groups gives no usable perspective: do not rely on CSS 3D. An icosahedron glenz at 48 frames is about 20-40 KB.

**Do not copy / caveats.** These effects are generic and free to use. Do not lift an object model that doubles as a group's mascot, or the surrounding logos. Removed as unverified: a jelly-cubes cracktro and a dots-on-sine-curves 10k intro that the submitted research cited without links, and the often-repeated claim that Second Reality is the PC reference for glenz (Wikipedia's article on it names no glenz). The red balls in Deviance's 2006 cracktro are called bouncing spheres by commenters, not glenz. Keep rotation slow; flickering translucent faces are unpleasant at header size, and the goo filter can stutter on phones.

## pc-14 · Texture movers: rotozoomer and tunnel

**Animated SVG** · build: **Medium** · impact: **3/5** · Mid-90s chunky-pixel effects; in PC cracktros 1996-2007 (Hybrid with SAC, Halcyon for Paradigm on DOS; Paradox with Chemical Reaction, Razor, Genesis on Windows)

Two effects that fill the frame by moving a texture rather than an object. The rotozoomer spins and scales a tiled image about the centre; the tunnel maps a texture onto the inside of an endless tube using precomputed distance and angle tables, so shifting the lookups makes you fly forward while the walls rotate. Both were standard ways to fill the space behind a logo, and commenters are blunt when they are done badly (Razor's 2001 rotozoomer is called slow and blocky, Paradox's 2000 tunnel 'disgusting' but charming).

**What it looks like**

- Rotozoomer: one small tile repeated to infinity, rotating and breathing in and out so its grid lines sweep across the screen; a grey embossed tile under a pastel logo decorated with pixel fruit in Hybrid's Alien Incident cracktro
- A metallic figure texture rotozoomed in a window beside the text block, with ornate metal bars top and bottom (Razor Blue Shift cracktro, 2001)
- Tunnel with a perforated wall: a blue-grey tube punched with round holes, a row of glowing blue lights receding into it and a rough golden rock hanging in the centre (Halcyon for Paradigm, Rogue Squadron cracktro)
- Nested-squares tunnel: about a dozen white-outlined squares, each rotated a little more than the last, shrinking to a dark centre on black, with lines of white text set at odd angles in perspective around it (Razor's ViRiLiTY 2000th cracktro, 2003)
- Blurred feedback tunnel in murky green behind a teal bevelled logo and a dashed text block (Paradox/Chemical Reaction cracktro; commenters praise the feedback and dislike the blur)
- Checker tunnel and '3D plasma tunnel' in a 4k: green/teal checker walls, then concentric red plasma rings (Genesis Cracktro \#3, 2007)
- Text sits on top in a plain block or on a solid panel; the effect is the background

**Palette.** Rotozoomers use two-tone tiles (grey on grey, metal). Tunnels are usually one cool hue darkening toward the centre (blue-grey, teal, murky green), stark white lines on black for the nested-squares kind, or red-orange rings for the plasma tunnel.

**Lettering.** The host cracktro's bitmap font over the effect, with an outline or a solid text panel for legibility.

**Motion.** Rotozoomer: steady rotation with a slower zoom oscillation and a drifting centre. Tunnel: constant forward motion, slow roll, the vanishing point wandering on a sine path (mechanism per Lode Vandevenne's tutorial).

**As a README header.** Rotozoomer: an 800x160 band of rotating, zooming tiles made from the project's own monogram, with the wordmark on a solid strip above and text below. Tunnel: a full-width strip of nested squares or rings rushing outward with the project name on a solid plate in the centre. Either also works as the background layer of another style here.

**How to build it.** Rotozoomer is easy (researcher tested in Chromium): a &lt;pattern&gt; whose patternTransform carries two SMIL animateTransform elements, rotate plus scale with additive=sum, gives a true rotozoom in under 1 KB with any vector tile. Tunnel: a texture-mapped tunnel needs per-pixel lookups and is not possible, but the nested-shape tunnel is: 12-16 squares or rings, each scaling from 0 to beyond the frame on one shared keyframe with evenly staggered negative delays and an exponential timing curve, vector-effect non-scaling-stroke or stroke width keyed to scale, a small rotation offset per ring, and the group's centre on a slow sine path; a radial gradient overlay darkens the centre. Firefox and Safari behaviour of the animated patternTransform is unverified.

**Do not copy / caveats.** Generic effects, free to use; do not reuse the textures or logos of the cited cracktros. Dates conflict between databases for two references (Rogue Squadron: Feb vs 29 Nov 1998; Paradox: May 2000 vs 2002). The genesis 4k's music is called a rip of an Amiga tune by commenters, unrelated to the visuals. These are the effects most likely to cause motion discomfort: keep rotation under about 45 degrees per second, confine them to a band, and freeze under prefers-reduced-motion.
