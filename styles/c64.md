# C64, Amiga and Atari ST

## Reviewer's summary

Checked against the sources, the 8-bit and 16-bit side holds up as a family of machine-specific schools, and all eleven submitted styles survive with corrections. I added three that the research skipped and the owner or brief asked for: an Amiga-era Razor 1911 cracktro school (the Sector9 intros of 1990-91), bobs and dot objects, and the copper-only screen.

C64 (about 1986-92): black 320x200 screen, 16 fixed colours, 8x8 character cells, 24x21 sprites. Logos are character mosaics or expanded sprites, colour comes from raster splits by scanline, motion is per character (DYCP). The same cells give PETSCII pictures and disk-directory art, the one pure-text style here.

Amiga (about 1988-93): 32 colours from 4096, plus a copper that changes colour per scanline. It runs from megademo and trainer menus, through single-effect screens (vertical "Kefrens" bars, bob chains, copper-only colour fields), to the 1992 design break of Melon Dezign's flat colour and Spaceballs' silhouettes. The hand-pixelled chrome logo is a separate static tradition.

Atari ST (about 1988-91): 16 colours from 512, so it leans on per-scanline palette changes, very large gradient scroller fonts and digitised pictures, in UK compilation "menu disks" and in megademos with game-like menus.

On the owner's questions. Razor 1911: their Amiga years are in this family and are now a style; the better-known PC-era Razor look belongs to the PC family. Chiptune: within this family it can only be the silent track-list jukebox. Vaporwave and NFO ASCII artists are outside this family.

Most styles translate well to SVG because they are flat rectangles, stepped gradients and sine motion. Web search ran out part-way, so the "graffiti" pixel-logo school named in the brief could not be sourced and is left out instead of written from memory.

## c64-01 · C64 one-screen crack intro: raster-split logo and text on colour bars

**Animated SVG** · build: **Easy** · impact: **3/5** · 1986-1989, Commodore 64 cracking scene (Europe, plus NTSC intros from the US such as Eagle Soft); small intros placed in front of cracked games

The single-screen C64 intro: a group logo at the top, a few centred lines naming the game, cracker and date, and a scroller, all on black. Commenters on the C64 intro database call the Fairlight one "one of the most used intros ever" and the Eagle Soft one "THE C64-intro". It differs from the existing C64 boot/loader style: no BASIC screen and no loading stripes, only the intro itself.

**What it looks like**

- Pure black screen and border, everything centred on a 40-column grid
- Logo as one flat shape whose colour changes in horizontal bands by scanline: Dynamic Duo's two-row logo in red/green/yellow/blue bands; Ikari's upright slab capitals in blue and yellow bands
- Logo drawn as an outline over a block of raster stripes: Fairlight's thin pink outlined letters sit on green/white stripes between two red rules
- Logo as a mosaic of small bevelled squares: Triad's grey block-grid letters, about 6 character rows high, with one block blinking
- Full-width raster bars about 8 pixels tall behind single text lines, shaded in 3-5 luminance steps (Fairlight: a pink bar behind the game title, a blue bar behind the 'CRACKED ... BY ...' line)
- Fixed text block in 8x8 capitals: 'PRESENTS', game title, 'CRACKED ON dd/mm/yy BY handle', often fenced with dashes or dollar signs
- One scroller near the bottom in double-width characters, plus optional 'PRESS SPACE'
- Variants: brick-textured block letters on a blue panel above an orange brick wall panel with big yellow letters (Hotline); a multicolour bitmap picture panel in place of the logo with one grey label bar and a scroller under it (Eagle Soft)

**Palette.** The 16 fixed VIC-II colours on black. Pepto's measured values: black 000000, white FFFFFF, red 68372B, cyan 70A4B2, purple 6F3D86, green 588D43, blue 352879, yellow B8C76F, orange 6F4F25, brown 433900, light red 9A6759, dark grey 444444, grey 6C6C6C, light green 9AD284, light blue 6C5EB5, light grey 959595. Colours share luminance levels (blue = brown, red = dark grey, purple = orange, light blue = grey, green = light red, cyan = light grey, yellow = light green), so bar shading and fades run in ramps such as blue, light blue, cyan, white or brown, orange, yellow, white.

**Lettering.** 8x8-pixel uppercase character sets, the ROM font or a redrawn one (thin 'computer' capitals, slab capitals). Logos are character mosaics or sprites doubled in X and Y, so edges step in 8-pixel or 2-pixel units. Scroller fonts are 1x1, 1x2 or 2x2 characters. Almost no lower case.

**Motion.** Raster colours roll vertically through the logo and the text bars; the scroller moves right to left at constant speed; one element may blink. Everything else is static. Motion is taken from user comments (colour rotation on Dynamic Duo, the logo blink on Triad); only stills were viewed.

**As a README header.** Above the fold: one animated SVG about 640x240 (2x scale of 320x120). Project name as the block logo with rolling raster bands, then PRESENTS, a one-line description on a raster bar, and a 'CRACKED ON' style line reused for version and date. The scroller carries the tagline or stats. Real links go in normal Markdown directly under the image.

**How to build it.** Build the logo from rects on an 8-pixel grid and use it as a clipPath over a tall stack of palette-coloured stripes that translates vertically in a CSS @keyframes loop with steps() timing; that is the raster split. Text bars are rects whose fill steps through 4-5 palette colours. The scroller is one group translated on X with the text duplicated for a seamless loop. No font can load, so define the 8x8 glyphs once as symbol elements and place them with use; about 60 glyphs costs a few KB. shape-rendering: crispEdges keeps the pixels hard.

**Do not copy / caveats.** Do not copy any group name, logo shape or the Eagle Soft picture; the layout, raster split and block-letter technique are generic. Period scrolltexts often contain slurs (the Fairlight still shows one), so write new text. Dates are those shown on the intro screens and were not cross-checked on CSDb. Partly overlaps the 'SID title screen' part of the existing C64 style; keep this one strictly to the crack-intro layout.

## c64-02 · C64 DYCP demo part: sine-wave letters under a chunky multicolour logo

**Animated SVG** · build: **Medium** · impact: **4/5** · 1989-1992, Commodore 64 demo scene (one-file demos, later crack intros)

DYCP ("Different Y Character Position") is the C64 scroller in which every character sits at its own height, so a line of text ripples along a sine curve as it scrolls. It belongs with FLD (whole rows bouncing), tech-tech (each line of a logo shifted sideways) and sprites or scrollers in the opened border as the signature tricks of 1989-90.

**What it looks like**

- A large two-row logo in multicolour character graphics with double-wide pixels, each letter filled with diagonal or vertical colour stripes in 3-4 palette steps and a dark outline (Sphinx: diagonal rainbow stripes)
- The logo sits on a mauve panel made of thin horizontal stripes, closed above and below by grey/white raster rules
- Text whose letters each ride a sine wave, forming an arc or U shape under the logo (The Force: letters hanging in a U beneath a two-row logo, yellow top row, blue-grey metallic bottom row)
- A small static credit line in a second colour ('CODE BY ... MUSIC BY ...') at the very top
- A straight one-line message in a gold gradient font between logo rows
- Optional open-border variant: the scroller or sprites run in the top and bottom border area, outside the 320x200 picture

**Palette.** C64 16-colour palette on black. Logo fills use luminance ramps (blue, light blue, cyan, white; brown, orange, yellow, white). Multicolour mode means 160 double-wide pixels across, so logo edges step in 2-pixel units.

**Lettering.** Logo: fat squared capitals about 4-5 character cells high. Scroller: 8x8 custom capitals, one colour per character, sometimes with a raster colour wash.

**Motion.** Each letter moves up and down on a sine table, offset in phase from its neighbour, while the whole line scrolls left. FLD: the entire logo bounces vertically. Tech-tech: each scanline of the logo slides horizontally. Motion comes from written descriptions, not from watching.

**As a README header.** Above the fold: an animated SVG with the project name as the striped logo on a mauve panel and the tagline as the DYCP line beneath it. Keep the wave amplitude modest (8-12 pixels at 1x) so the line stays readable. Stats or section names go in the static credit line. Links sit in Markdown below.

**How to build it.** Each glyph is a use of an 8x8 symbol inside its own group with a CSS translateY animation (animation-direction: alternate, timing cubic-bezier(0.37,0,0.63,1), which is a near-exact sine) and a negative animation-delay that grows per character. One translateX animation on the parent scrolls the line; duplicate the text and make the per-glyph phase repeat over the text length so the loop is seamless. 40-80 animated glyphs is well within budget. FLD is one translateY on the logo group. Tech-tech is the logo cut into 1-row clip strips with staggered translateX. The open-border variant is only a drawn C64 border frame with the scroller placed over it.

**Do not copy / caveats.** Do not copy the group logos or their charsets; sine text and striped block letters are generic. No source opened says who did DYCP first, so make no such claim. Moving text is harder to read, so repeat the real information as static text. The open-border variant loses its meaning outside a real screen and only reads as a C64 in-joke.

**References**

- [DYCP Intro by The Force (1991, C64 crack intro, code Nir P.): scroller arcing under the logo](<https://csdb.dk/release/?id=104753>)
- [DYCP in 1992 by Chaos-Front (March 1992, one-file demo, code and charset Nuke)](<https://csdb.dk/release/?id=249976>)

## c64-03 · PETSCII picture: C64 text-mode art on the 40-column grid

**Static SVG** · build: **Medium** · impact: **3/5** · Character set used on the C64 from 1982; a demoscene art form in its own right since Marq's PETSCII editor of September 2013

> Also researched as [ansi-10](ansi.md#ansi-10).

Pictures and logos built only from the C64's built-in character set: 40x25 cells, each an 8x8 glyph in one foreground colour over a single shared background colour. Tommi Musturi's article says it was first used for floppy directory art, BBS graphics and beginners' BASIC programs, and that competitions and dedicated demos came only after the editor made it available to everyone.

**What it looks like**

- A visible 8x8 cell grid: edges are horizontal, vertical or 45-degree diagonals, because those are the shapes the glyph set offers
- One background colour for the whole picture, plus one colour per cell
- Solid blocks, half and quarter blocks, diagonal wedges, line and corner glyphs, and the checkerboard glyph for the little dithering that works
- Reverse-video cells used to double the glyph set (128 glyphs plus 128 inverses)
- Deliberately visible colour clashes between neighbouring cells, giving a comic-strip look
- A wide single-colour border around the 320x200 picture area (light violet in the 2026 example viewed, which shows a lawn, trees, two seated figures and a computer on a table)
- Lettering made from the same glyphs: ROM capitals, or large letters assembled from block and wedge cells 3-5 cells tall

**Palette.** The 16 fixed C64 colours; one global background plus one colour per cell. The example viewed uses a bright green ground, light violet sky and border, with yellow, brown, black and white on top.

**Lettering.** C64 ROM character set (uppercase plus graphics symbols). Big letters are mosaics of block, wedge and line glyphs.

**Motion.** Usually static. When animated it is cell changes frame by frame (a blinking cursor, colour cycling on a few cells, a two-frame character swap), never smooth movement.

**As a README header.** Above the fold: a static or lightly animated SVG on a 40-column grid, 12-16 rows tall so it works as a banner: project name in block-glyph letters, a small scene or mascot built from cells, a coloured border. A caption line in ROM-style capitals gives the tagline. Body text and links stay in Markdown.

**How to build it.** Define each glyph used as an 8x8 symbol (runs of rects), place cells with use and a fill colour, draw the background as one rect, set shape-rendering to crispEdges. 40x14 cells is 560 elements, roughly 30-40 KB. A few cells can swap or change colour with steps() keyframes. The technique is easy; drawing a good picture within it is the hard part, so a generator should offer block-letter titles and a small library of original cell motifs. A pure-text version is not dependable: Unicode's Symbols for Legacy Computing block has PETSCII-like glyphs but common monospace fonts lack them, so only ordinary block and box-drawing characters are safe on GitHub.

**Do not copy / caveats.** Do not trace or copy anyone's PETSCII picture. The glyph shapes come from Commodore's ROM font; redraw equivalent 8x8 shapes instead of ripping the font. Low-contrast C64 pairs (blue on black, brown on black) fail contrast checks, so put text on strong pairs. Wow depends heavily on the picture; a title-only banner is a 3.

**References**

- [Tommi Musturi, 'Unplanned Blocky Puzzles: Creating PETSCII for the Commodore 64' (WiderScreen, 2017): constraints, global-colour strategy, dithering limits, history of use](<https://widerscreen.fi/numerot/2017-1-2/unplanned-blocky-puzzles-creating-petscii-for-the-commodore-64/>)
- [Marq's PETSCII editor on CSDb (Fit, 28 September 2013; help from Dr. TerrorZ)](<https://csdb.dk/release/?id=122086>)
- [C64-Wiki on the editor: 40x25 with 16 colours, a 16x25 dir-art mode, export formats](<https://www.c64-wiki.com/wiki/Marq's_PETSCII_editor>)

## c64-04 · C64 disk directory art (dir art)

**Text / ASCII** · build: **Easy** · impact: **3/5** · C64 floppy disks from the 1980s onward; still made, with tools and competition entries on CSDb dated 2019-2026

Art drawn inside a 1541 disk directory listing: when the disk is listed, the file names themselves form a picture or a framed title. It works because directory listings on a C64 are unsorted and the directory blocks are simple enough to edit by hand, so an artist can fill the 16-character name column with PETSCII graphics row after row. Unlike the existing boot/loader style, the listing itself is the canvas.

**What it looks like**

- A reverse-video header line: a zero, the disk name in quotes, then a short ID (in the example viewed the ID slot carries the artist's tag)
- Every following row has three columns: a block count on the left (usually 0), a quoted 16-character name, and a file type on the right (PRG or DEL)
- The art lives only in the 16-character name column, so the picture is a narrow vertical strip 16 cells wide and as many rows tall as needed
- Line-drawing and block glyphs form a framed title, big outline lettering and a small scene (the example shows a title in double-line letters, a crane, two rooftops and a standing figure)
- A footer line: a number followed by BLOCKS FREE.
- One colour only: light blue-violet text on a darker blue-violet screen, the default C64 colours
- A signature in the last name row

**Palette.** Monochrome by nature: default C64 light blue (6C5EB5) on blue (352879). As README text it takes the reader's theme colours.

**Lettering.** C64 ROM capitals and PETSCII line, corner and block glyphs; fixed 16-character name field; the quotes around each name are part of the look.

**Motion.** static

**As a README header.** Entirely text in a pre block. The header line is the project name, each 'file' row is either a row of the picture or a real entry (a section name that can be a link inside the pre), the left column carries real numbers (item counts, sizes), and the footer is a stat line. Illustration of the row structure, my own: a header such as 0 "FACTORY TOOLS   " 2A, rows such as 140  "ITEMS"  PRG and 0  "+--------------+"  DEL, then 477 BLOCKS FREE. An optional SVG twin can add the blue screen.

**How to build it.** About 30 columns wide, far below 80. Use only box-drawing and block characters every monospace font has. GitHub's pre line spacing leaves small gaps in vertical strokes, so designs built from horizontal strokes and solid blocks survive better than tall thin frames. Anchor tags inside the pre make name rows clickable. Reverse video for the header is not available in plain text; mark it with a rule line or bold instead.

**Do not copy / caveats.** Do not copy an existing directory picture; the listing format is a machine convention and free to imitate. Do not reproduce Commodore's startup banner wording as branding. Screen readers read the rows character by character, so put a plain-text summary under it. This is the only pure-text style in this family, which is its main value.

- [Championship Wrestling +3D DirArt (Excess, 24 February 2019; concept Faayd and Logiker, directory art Logiker; 3rd in C64 graphics at BCC Party \#13)](<https://csdb.dk/release/?id=175500>)

## c64-05 · Razor 1911 Amiga cracktro (Sector9, 1990-91): logo plate, deep-blue panel, rainbow copper text

**Text / ASCII + SVG** · build: **Easy** · impact: **4/5** · 1990-1991, Amiga OCS; cracktros and text screens by the Norwegian group's Amiga section, mostly credited to Sector9

Razor 1911 began on the C64 in 1985 as Razor 2992 and moved to the Amiga in 1987-88, before its PC years. Pouet lists about fifteen Amiga cracktros from 1989 to 1994. 'Birds of Prey' (December 1991, code and graphics Sector9, with Sim; music 'Necroscope' by Mantronix) is the one commenters call "a great famous crack intro by the REAL razor 1911", praising its chiptune and "wavy colour cycling with the swirling letters". It is a sub-variant of the existing Amiga cracktro: no starfield and no copper-bar stack, but a framed two-panel layout in which the text itself carries the colour.

**What it looks like**

- Two stacked panels on black, separated by thin light-blue rules: a short mid-grey strip on top, a tall deep navy panel below
- In the grey strip, a small logo plate: a dark rounded capsule with a chrome rim holding the group name in red outlined capitals with a white highlight, and a small bar ornament either side
- In the navy panel, 3 lines of wide, slanted, rounded capitals whose fill is a horizontal rainbow running red, orange, yellow, green, cyan, blue across the line
- Dashed rules above and below the text made of short segments coloured in the same rainbow order
- Small four-point star sprites in light blue at both ends of the first text line
- Text-page variant (Rolling Ronny, July 1991; Vertical Insanity, 1990): a full dark-blue screen of tightly packed 8-pixel capitals in blocks separated by dashed rules (title, supplied by / cracked by, contacts in two columns, greetings), each block tinted a different colour (magenta, white, orange, light blue)
- Black-screen variant (Renegade Legion Interceptor, May 1991): centred blue-gradient serif text lines between '--=))' and '((=--' ornaments, red chrome letters that spin into place, and a one-line orange-gradient scroller on a navy band with thin rules at the bottom

**Palette.** Amiga 12-bit colour. Navy panel (about 002 to 005 in 12-bit terms), mid-grey strip, light-blue rules, a full-hue rainbow for the text, red with white highlights for the logo. The text-page variant uses magenta, white, orange and light blue on dark blue.

**Lettering.** Wide slanted rounded capitals about 16 pixels high for the rainbow lines; an 8-pixel capitals font for the text pages; a heavy red outlined wordmark with white highlights for the logo. All hand-pixelled by the group's own artists (Pouet and Demozoo credit Sector9 and, for one logo, Insane TTM).

**Motion.** The rainbow cycles along the text and the lines wave; commenters describe swirling letters. In the black-screen variant letters rotate into place and a scroller runs at the bottom. Text pages are static apart from colour cycling. Motion is from comments, not from watching.

**As a README header.** Above the fold: an animated SVG about 800x300. Top strip with the project name in a capsule plate; navy panel with three rainbow lines: project name, PRESENTS, the one-line description; dashed rainbow rules and two star sprites. Under it, the text-page variant as a real pre block: title, 'supplied by / built by', contacts, greetings, which doubles as the README's info section with working links.

**How to build it.** Rainbow text: pixel-glyph shapes as a clipPath over a wide horizontal linearGradient with hard stops (12-bit steps), twice the panel width and translated on X in a loop so the colours cycle. The wave: cut each line into per-glyph groups with translateY, alternate direction, cubic-bezier(0.37,0,0.63,1) and staggered negative delays. Dashed rules are short rects with the same gradient. Stars are 5-rect sprites with an opacity blink. The logo plate is a rounded rect with a stepped gradient rim. The text page needs no SVG at all.

**Do not copy / caveats.** This is the reference most at risk of becoming an imitation: do not use the Razor 1911 name, the numerals, the capsule wordmark or its letterforms, and do not imitate their release wording as if the project were theirs. The reusable parts are the two-panel frame, rainbow-filled wavy text and dashed rules. The owner may have meant the PC-era Razor intros, which are a different look and another family. Rainbow colour cycling is a motion and contrast risk; keep it slow and add a prefers-reduced-motion rule. Period text pages carry phone numbers and postal addresses; never reproduce them.

- [Birds of Prey on Demozoo: 7 December 1991; Sector 9 code and graphics, Sim code, Mantronix music ('Necroscope')](<https://demozoo.org/productions/22152/>)

## c64-06 · Amiga megademo menu and trainer menu: chrome logo, giant scroller, dotted-leader option list

**Text / ASCII + SVG** · build: **Medium** · impact: **3/5** · 1989-1990 for megademos (Red Sector, Kefrens, Alcatraz); 1989 onward for trainer menus in front of cracked games

Two related Amiga list screens. ## c64-07 · Vertical copper bars ('Kefrens bars' / 'Alcatraz bars'): ribbons twisting down the screen

**Animated SVG** · build: **Medium** · impact: **4/5** · 1989-1991 on Amiga; seen on the Atari ST by 1991

A one-trick Amiga effect: shaded vertical bars that sway left and right as they descend, leaving a woven ribbon pattern. It is made by showing a single line of video memory on every scanline and drawing a new bar into that line as the beam moves down. It carries Kefrens' name, but a Pouet commenter (hitchhikr) says it was invented by Metalwar of Alcatraz in 'We are first' in Megademo 3 (February 1989).

**What it looks like**

- Vertical bars: each a narrow column 8-16 pixels wide with a rounded shading ramp, dark edges and a bright centre
- Each bar starts at its own scanline and runs to the bottom, so later bars overlap earlier ones and the top of the screen is emptier than the bottom
- Bar positions follow a sine from one scanline to the next, giving S-curves and braided ribbons
- Visible one-line steps along the curves, because a bar can only shift by whole pixels
- Black background; bars in two or three hues
- Usually paired with a very large scroller in front; in Alcatraz Megademo 3 the big letters are built from a grey stone-block texture
- Atari ST version: yellow and blue stepped bars spinning over a red-to-black scanline gradient, with letters made of dots rotating in front (Pompey Pirates Menu 001 V2)

**Palette.** Amiga 12-bit colours in ramps of 5-8 steps per bar, for example dark red to yellow to white and back, or deep blue to cyan to white. Commenters describe Razor 1911's 'Vertical Insanity' as a vertical copper rainbow.

**Lettering.** None of its own; paired with a large block-capital scroller.

**Motion.** The whole ribbon pattern swings and twists continuously as the sine phases advance. Steady and hypnotic, not fast. Not watched; taken from the technique description and comments.

**As a README header.** Above the fold: an animated SVG banner, wide and short (for example 800x200), with the ribbon filling the background and the project name in large block capitals in front on a dark plate so it stays readable. All information text stays outside the effect.

**How to build it.** Reproduce the real mechanism. For each of 60-100 scanline rows k, draw one rect that starts at row k and runs to the bottom, filled with a shared horizontal shading gradient, and animate its X with translateX (alternate, cubic-bezier(0.37,0,0.63,1)) using a negative delay proportional to k. Later rects paint over earlier ones, which gives the correct overlap for free. Nest a second group with a slower animation for the sum of two sines. About 100 animated elements and two gradients is a few KB.

**Do not copy / caveats.** The effect is a generic technique; do not attach the Kefrens, Alcatraz or Razor 1911 names to it as branding. The origin claim rests on one commenter repeated on two Pouet pages and should be presented as that. Continuous swaying across the full width can be uncomfortable: add a prefers-reduced-motion rule that freezes the ribbon.

- [Razor 1911 'Vertical Insanity' (August 1990): comments on the vertical copper rainbow, and hitchhikr's attribution to Metalwar of Alcatraz](<https://www.pouet.net/prod.php?which=4286>)

## c64-08 · Bobs and dot objects: a sphere chain on a Lissajous path, a rotating dot cube

**Animated SVG** · build: **Medium** · impact: **4/5** · 1989-1990, Amiga OCS; 'bobs' are blitter-drawn sprites, the staple effect of early Amiga intros

A black screen with one moving object made of many identical pieces: a chain of shaded balls snaking along a figure-eight, or a lattice of dots rotating in space, above a logo strip. Razor 1911's 'Liquid Bobs' (3rd at the IT, Rawhead, The Band and IMP-666 Megaparty 1989) and 'Space Dots' (January 1990) are compact examples, and Pouet commenters single out the vectorballs part of Red Sector's Megademo.

**What it looks like**

- About 30 shaded spheres in one hue (blue with a white highlight dot at upper left) strung along a figure-eight or infinity-shaped curve
- Sphere size varies along the chain, large near the viewer and small far away, so the loop reads as three-dimensional
- A lattice of single white-blue dots forming a box in perspective, rows of dots converging to one side (Space Dots)
- The effect area is closed above and below by a one-pixel horizontal rule whose colour runs through a rainbow from left to right
- A logo strip at the bottom: wide pixel capitals filled with a vertical gradient (violet to cyan in Space Dots; red over blue chrome with a white horizon in Liquid Bobs)
- Nothing else: black background, no starfield, no picture

**Palette.** Black; one ball colour in a 4-5 step ramp from dark blue to white; a rainbow hairline; a two-colour gradient logo. Amiga 12-bit steps.

**Lettering.** Only the logo strip: geometric pixel capitals 24-32 pixels high with gradient fill; Archmage's Pouet comment on Space Dots praises the font.

**Motion.** The chain travels along its curve, each ball following the one ahead; the curve itself slowly changes shape as the two sine frequencies drift. The dot box rotates. Shade bobs, a later relative in which overlapping bobs brighten the pixels beneath them (Demozoo's earliest tagged examples are December 1991), leave trails that cannot be reproduced without scripts.

**As a README header.** Above the fold: an animated SVG about 800x260. The sphere chain loops in the upper two thirds between two rainbow hairlines; the project name is the gradient logo strip at the bottom; a one-line tagline in small pixel capitals sits under it. For a factory tool the balls could be items riding the curve like a conveyor. All other text is Markdown.

**How to build it.** A Lissajous path needs no script: nest two groups per ball, the outer animating translateX and the inner translateY, each alternate with cubic-bezier(0.37,0,0.63,1) and different durations (for example 4s and 2s gives a figure-eight). Give each of 30 balls a negative delay offset to form the chain. A third nested animation on scale, a quarter period out of phase, gives depth. Each ball is a circle with one shared radialGradient using hard stops. A dot lattice rotating about the vertical axis is exact in the same way: each dot's X is a sine of time with its own amplitude and phase, and opacity follows a second sine for depth. Z-order cannot change during the loop, a small inaccuracy. 30 balls or 60 dots is a few KB.

**Do not copy / caveats.** Do not copy the Razor 1911 logo strips or names; balls on a sine path and dot lattices are generic. The two databases disagree on the Liquid Bobs date and credits, as shown. Only stills were seen, so the motion description is inferred. True shade bobs and filled or glenz vector objects are not achievable without scripts beyond a few pre-computed frames.

- [Liquid Bobs on Demozoo: dated 26 March 1989; Doctor No code, Sector 9 code and charset, Insane TTM the Razor logo, Hattrick music; tag 'bobs'](<https://demozoo.org/productions/2184/>)

## c64-09 · Copper-only screen: full-screen colour fields with no bitmap at all

**Animated SVG** · build: **Medium** · impact: **4/5** · 1990-1991, Amiga OCS

Screens drawn with the Amiga's copper alone: the display list changes the background colour every scanline and several times along each line, so the picture is pure colour with no pixels behind it. Angels' 'Copper Master' and Razor 1911's 'Look! No bitplanes' were both released at the No Limits and IMP-666 party in October 1990. Plasma, which Wikipedia notes was particularly common on the Amiga because it could be done very efficiently there, is the best-known relative.

**What it looks like**

- Soft diagonal bands sweeping across the whole screen in three or four ramps (green, brown, blue, white)
- A diamond of nested colour: red at the edges through orange and yellow to a blue core, mirrored top to bottom and left to right
- A plaid of overlapping vertical and horizontal bars in pure red, green and blue whose crossings mix to yellow, cyan and magenta
- A twisted ribbon made of thin alternating red and grey stripes over a field of cyan horizontal lines
- Colour changes only in chunky steps along a line (cells several pixels wide) but on every scanline vertically, so the texture is wide and short
- Each effect shown in a bordered window with a thin light frame; no logo art, at most a plain text title
- In 'Look! No bitplanes': a big red wordmark with jagged serifs and blue chrome lines of text on black, plus what a commenter calls a copper starfield

**Palette.** The whole 12-bit range at once: fully saturated red, green and blue and their mixes, in ramps of 16 steps per channel. A Pouet commenter on Copper Master says he had not seen so many colours in a long while.

**Lettering.** Minimal. Copper Master credits Mistervideo for font and design (Demozoo: Mr. Video, charset); titles are plain capitals.

**Motion.** Bands roll, the diamond pulses outward, plaid bars slide across each other, the ribbon twists. Plasma-style movement comes from summing sine waves and cycling the palette. Copper Master's coder commented that matching the colours to Moby's music took a long time. Not watched; from stills and written sources.

**As a README header.** Above the fold: an animated SVG banner that is nothing but moving colour, with the project name in large solid capitals (black or white with a dark outline) on top and a one-line tagline. It carries its own background, so it works in light and dark themes. Everything informative is Markdown below.

**How to build it.** Rolling bands: a stack of 2-pixel-high rects sharing one fill animation through a 16-step palette, each row with a negative delay proportional to its row number. Plaid: two groups of gradient-filled bars, one vertical and one horizontal, translated in opposite directions with mix-blend-mode: screen on the upper group (or an feBlend filter). Plasma: a grid of about 40x12 cells; precompute a sum-of-sines value per cell, quantise it to 16 or 32 levels, give every cell of one level the same class, and run one colour-cycle animation per class with the delay set by the level. That is palette cycling and needs no script; 480 rects is about 20 KB. Keep cells 8 pixels wide and 2-4 tall for the copper look.

**Do not copy / caveats.** The effects are generic; do not reuse the group names or the Razor wordmark. Full-screen saturated colour cycling is the biggest accessibility risk in this family: keep cycles slower than about one per 3 seconds, avoid red flashing, and freeze it under prefers-reduced-motion. Text over it needs a solid plate or heavy outline to stay legible. I did not confirm that 'Look! No bitplanes' contains a plasma, so none is claimed for it.

- [Copper Master on Demozoo: 6 October 1990, No Limits and IMP-666 Amiga Conference; note that Corsair later made a similar-looking crack intro for Angels](<https://demozoo.org/productions/123/>)
- [Look! No bitplanes by Razor 1911 (October 1990, 3rd at the same party; code Nosferatu, graphics Sector9)](<https://www.pouet.net/prod.php?which=4634>)

- One human silhouette, large and centred, arms spread wide, with no interior detail
- A background of fine concentric rings from two offset centres whose overlap makes a moire interference pattern of crescents and swirls

**As a README header.** Above the fold: an animated SVG with a bold silhouette of the project's subject (for a factory tool a machine, crane or gear, not a person) recoloured out of slowly crawling moire rings, and the project name in plain heavy capitals on a solid strip at the bottom. ## c64-12 · 16-bit hand-pixelled chrome logo (static)

**Static SVG** · build: **Medium** · impact: **4/5** · 1988-1993, Amiga and Atari ST; drawn in Deluxe Paint at 320 pixels wide with 16 or 32 colours

The group logo as a piece of pixel art: metal letters reflecting an imaginary sky and desert, drawn dot by dot. Damien Guard's article on Amiga lettering describes the chrome, gradient and lens-flare conventions within 16 or 32 colours, and the book 'Demoscene: The Logo Art' is devoted to logos and their makers. Named hands exist: Demozoo credits Uno with the red and blue logos and charsets in Phenomena's 'Enigma' (1991), and the Amiga Graphics Archive documents a TRSi logo by J.O.E. in 32 colours, 19 used.

**What it looks like**

- Chrome fill split by a horizon about halfway up each letter: cool colours above (violet or deep blue to pale blue to white), a hard zigzag line, warm colours below (dark red to orange to yellow, or grey)
- Banded, stepped gradients of 5-8 colours, because the whole logo shares a 16- or 32-colour palette
- A one-pixel dark outline around every letter, often with a lighter inner edge to fake a bevel
- A solid drop shadow or extruded side offset down and to the right
- Heavy italic or slab capitals, letters touching or overlapping
- Single-hue variant (Enigma): hot red to pink letters cut by thin horizontal slits with white streak highlights, beside a violet chrome wordmark with a white horizon
- Composite title variant (Red Sector Megademo): a second word set vertically beside the main logo, plus a cartoon head with a speech bubble
- Chrome with white sparkle stars on letter corners over a blue gradient floor (Kefrens 'Multi Megamix' title)

**Palette.** One cool ramp and one warm ramp plus black and white. Amiga colours are 12-bit, so each ramp step is one sixteenth per channel. On the ST the palette is 512 colours with 16 on screen, so ramps are shorter and coarser.

**Lettering.** Custom-drawn capitals only: wide letterforms with flat tops to give room for the horizon band, light from the upper left, highlights as one-pixel white lines along top edges, sparing checkerboard dither where two bands meet.

**Motion.** Static as an image. An optional glint can travel across the letters.

**As a README header.** Above the fold: a static SVG of the project name as a chrome pixel logo on black, 640-800 pixels wide, optionally with a one-line subtitle in small pixel capitals. It can stand alone or sit on top of any animated style in this family.

**How to build it.** Draw letters as pixel-grid rects (run-length merged) with shape-rendering: crispEdges at 2x or 3x. Fill through a clipPath with a linearGradient that uses paired hard stops for bands; add the zigzag horizon as a polyline clipped to the letters. Dither between bands with a 2x2 checkerboard pattern. Outline and shadow are offset copies of the letter shape. Sparkles are small 4-point polygons. An optional glint is a narrow skewed white rect animated across the clip. The SVG is simple; convincing letterforms are not, and a generated logo will look mechanical beside a hand-pixelled one.

**Do not copy / caveats.** Do not copy any existing group logo, the Red Sector head, or an artist's specific letterforms; the chrome recipe is common property. Artist attributions are only those stated by Demozoo and the Amiga Graphics Archive; which logo Imbecille drew in Enigma is not specified. Scene graphics of the period were often adapted from album covers and commercial art, another reason to draw from scratch. The brief also asked about 'graffiti' logos and other named logo artists; I could not source that school and have not described it.

## c64-13 · Atari ST menu disk: key-numbered game list, big gradient scroller, scanline rasters

**Text / ASCII + SVG** · build: **Easy** · impact: **4/5** · 1988-1991, Atari ST; the UK compilation scene (Automation, Medway Boys, Pompey Pirates) and the megademos whose tricks they borrowed

On the ST, UK crackers packed several games onto one floppy and put a menu in front. Zippy of the Medway Boys made more than 100 of them, starting with menu 9, and a Pouet commenter says of Automation's intros that "there are hundreds". Each menu is a small intro with a job: list the games, say which key loads which, and scroll the news. Megademos such as The Union Demo (January 1989) and The Carebears' Cuddly Demos (July 1989) supplied the effects and the game-like main menu.

**What it looks like**

- A short key list of the disk's contents: '1...GAME ONE, 2...GAME TWO' or 'F1: ...' to 'F4: ...', in small white or yellow capitals
- Utility keys in the same block: '0...TOGGLE 50/60 HZ', 'SPACE...READ DOC FILE', 'M TO CHANGE MUSIC'
- A disk number line ('DISK 10', 'CD-COMPACT NUMBER 190')
- A big scroller across the bottom in letters about 32 pixels high, each filled with a vertical gradient (white to pink, or orange to red) with a dark edge
- A centrepiece picture: a heavily dithered digitised photograph (a red and violet sunset sky in Pompey Pirates menu 10) or a cartoon character on a red oval (Automation 190)
- A group logo in warm gradient capitals, orange to red, with a drop shadow; a second panel of grey metal with a large orange word
- Full-width background gradients changed every scanline, red fading to black and back, banding in 8 steps
- Megademo-menu variant: a side-view platform-game scene of brick walls and a small character above a big chrome scroller (Cuddly Demos; Automation 496 does the same with a commercial game's graphics)

**Palette.** ST low resolution: 320x200, 16 colours on screen from 512, with more by switching the palette per scanline. 8 levels per channel, so gradients band strongly. Red-to-black backgrounds, orange and yellow logos, white small text, blue and yellow accents.

**Lettering.** Small text in a plain 8x8 capitals font, packed tightly at the top or bottom. Large scroller fonts are chunky capitals with a vertical gradient and a dark edge. Logos are hand-drawn slab capitals in orange and red.

**Motion.** The bottom scroller runs continuously; background rasters roll; a logo may distort line by line (commenters on Automation 190 praise the distortion); in one menu dot-built letters rotate over spinning vertical bars. The key list is static. From stills and comments.

**As a README header.** Above the fold: an animated SVG with a gradient logo, a picture or mascot panel, rolling red rasters and the big scroller. The key list maps directly to the README: '1...INSTALL, 2...USAGE, 3...RECIPES, SPACE...READ DOCS'. Put that list in real text under the image (a pre block with links) so the keys become clickable entries, and use the disk number for the version.

**How to build it.** Scanline rasters are a linearGradient with hard stops at 8 levels per channel, or a stack of 2-pixel rects; rolling them is one translateY loop. The scroller is pixel-glyph symbols clipped over a vertical gradient and translated on X. A 'digitised' picture can be faked without a bitmap by drawing flat shapes and overlaying an ordered-dither pattern; an embedded data: image is possible but heavy. The distorter is the logo cut into horizontal clip strips with staggered translateX animations. The key list is plain text.

**Do not copy / caveats.** Do not copy group names, logos, or the game graphics and cartoon characters some menus lifted from commercial games. The key-list structure is generic. ST border-breaking (overscan, fullscreen) is central to the machine's demo history but means nothing in an image with no screen edge, and the walkable megademo menu needs interaction, so neither is offered as its own style. The 'first fullscreen' claim is one Pouet comment, not a settled fact. '50/60 Hz' and similar keys are jokes that need a real meaning next to them.

## c64-14 · Chip-music jukebox: track list with times, embossed panel, 'hit a key' legend

**Text / ASCII + SVG** · build: **Easy** · impact: **3/5** · 1988-1991: Atari ST (The Exceptions' B.I.G. Demo, January 1988) and Amiga music disks (Kefrens' Jukebox, 29 June 1990; Multi Megamix, November 1991)

A screen whose whole purpose is choosing chip tunes. The B.I.G. Demo carried 113 YM2149 tunes by Jochen Hippel (Mad Max), over four hours of conversions of Rob Hubbard's C64 music, behind a menu that lists tune names with running times; Pouet commenters recall listening for hours. Amiga groups turned the idea into music disks with a drawn jukebox or a title picture. It is the closest this family gets to 'chiptune' without any sound.

**What it looks like**

- A scrolling track list in a window: tune name on the left, duration in minutes and seconds on the right, three or four rows visible
- The selected row drawn on a solid red bar, other rows in green and blue text
- A 'TOP OF LIST' header above the list and key instructions below in larger lettering ('Hit 1...3 for ...', 'Hit B for the ... scroller')
- ST version: a light grey embossed metal panel with title letters raised from the same grey by light and shadow edges, and round studs filled with multicolour dither
- A thin strip of rainbow-coloured blocks under the legend, and scrollers running in the area beneath the panel
- Amiga version (Kefrens Jukebox, graphics by Seen): a hand-pixelled jukebox with a pink-white arch, gold side columns and a brown cabinet, the current tune's title on a small display strip, party credits to the right, and a wide blue scroller band at the bottom
- Amiga title-picture version (Multi Megamix): a chrome wordmark with white sparkles, red oscilloscope traces behind it, and a second chrome logo on a blue gradient floor

**Palette.** ST: mid and light greys for the panel, red for the selection, green and blue list text, a rainbow strip as the only saturated element. Amiga: black background, chrome greys, gold, pink and brown for the jukebox, blue scroller.

**Lettering.** Embossed block capitals for titles; a small 8-pixel font for the list with times right-aligned; larger rounded lettering for the key legend.

**Motion.** The list scrolls when the selection moves; the selection bar steps row by row; scrollers run underneath; the rainbow strip cycles. A visual stand-in for playback (a ticking time counter, a pulsing level strip, a drawn oscilloscope trace) is the natural addition, though no level meter is visible in the stills.

**As a README header.** Above the fold: an animated SVG panel titled with the project name in embossed capitals, and a track list where each 'tune' is a module, command or release with a number on the right (size, count, duration). The highlighted row steps through the list and a 'now playing' line names the current item. Repeat the list as linked text below; the list with right-aligned times also works as a pre block.

**How to build it.** Emboss: three offset copies of each letter shape (light, dark, face). List: pixel-glyph rows; the selection bar is a rect moved with steps() keyframes. A ticking counter is a vertical strip of digits in a clip window stepped with steps(). A level strip is a handful of rects with scaleY keyframes at different durations. An oscilloscope trace is a polyline with 2-3 alternative point sets toggled by steps(). No sound is possible, so this only suggests music.

**Do not copy / caveats.** Do not copy the demo titles, group logos, tune names or the specific jukebox drawing. A track list with times and a highlight bar is generic. The B.I.G. Demo still is small, so fine details of its panel may be off. Embossed grey-on-grey lettering is low contrast; keep list text in a strong colour. A tracker pattern view, the more familiar chiptune image, already exists in the project's cracktro style and belongs to another family.

## Considered and left out

- None of the eleven submitted styles was removed. Each is distinct from the six existing styles, though two overlap them in part (the C64 crack intro with the existing 'SID title screen', the megademo menu with the existing Amiga cracktro) and are rated lower for it.
- Not added as its own style: Atari ST fullscreen and border-breaking megademo screens. There is no screen edge to break in an image and the walkable menu needs interaction. The reusable parts and the Union Demo and Cuddly Demos references are folded into the ST menu-disk style.
- Not added: C64 border-opening as its own style, for the same reason. It survives as an optional drawn-border variant in the DYCP style.
- Not added: the 'graffiti' pixel-logo school and further named logo artists from the brief. With web search exhausted I could not reach sources that describe it; the Amiga Graphics Archive pages I opened show pictures, not logos.
- Not added: shade bobs, glenz and filled vectors, and dot tunnels. Without scripts they need an accumulating buffer or many pre-computed frames. Only the dot-lattice and ball-chain forms, which reduce to sine motion, are in the bobs style.
