<h1 align="center">CASTAWAY</h1>

<p align="center">
  <b>A ten-hour lo-fi island video in which almost nothing happens, monitored around the clock.</b><br>
  <sub>Working title. In development. An unofficial remake inspired by <i>Johnny Castaway</i>, the 1992 desert-island screensaver.</sub>
</p>

<p align="center">
  <a href="tools/serve.py"><b>open the board</b></a> &nbsp;·&nbsp;

</p>

**Situation.** One tiny island. One tall palm. One raft. One young woman in cream headphones, nodding to the music, with a great deal of time. Every so often something happens: a message in a bottle washes straight back, a drone delivers a parcel that turns out to be more headphones, a stray cat arrives on a crate and naps up the palm, a coconut falls on a hermit crab and leaves with the crab inside. The rest of the time nothing happens, and that is the product. It is a stationary-frame video for YouTube in the spirit of the ten-hour lofi streams: 16:9, 1080p, 30 fps, sunny hand-painted coastal anime, always daytime.

**The board** up there replays one ten-hour run at 25 simulated minutes a second. Where and when each gag lands on it is made up; the counts are not. More than 90 activities live in [activities.toml](activities.toml) on four timers: regular every 2 to 5 minutes, occasional every 12 to 25, rare every 30 to 60, super rare every 3 to 6 hours. A typical run (the median of 200 simulated runs) comes to about 155, 30, 13 and 2 of them, plus chained follow-ups, and she is busy about a third of the time. Every activity starts on the next bar of the music, every 3 seconds, so the gags land on the beat. Lanes let things overlap, which is how a ship gets to cross the horizon while she is busy with something else. It waits for that. Watch the BUSY lamp.

**Sound.** Every sound is synthesized from code by [tools/make_audio.py](tools/make_audio.py): no samples, no loops, no recordings, so no third-party licence applies. There are more than 150 of them. The theme is a seamless 60-second loop at 80 BPM in F major (ii-V-I-vi, 20 bars of 3 seconds) with electric piano, a kalimba lead, soft drums and vinyl crackle, mixed to -14 LUFS with true peak at or below -1 dBTP. Nobody has listened to any of it yet. The board lists that as a known gap in coverage.

```sh
python tools/serve.py
# then open http://127.0.0.1:8765/
```

Live preview in the page, then export a YouTube-ready MP4: the browser encodes frame-exact H.264 with WebCodecs (68 to 78 frames a second at 1080p30 in Chrome), and the server mixes in the sound and joins the two. Plain ES modules, no build step, no npm packages. [`python tools/schedule.py`](tools/schedule.py) validates the schedule and simulates a ten-hour run; [`python tools/render_demo.py --dev`](tools/render_demo.py) renders a dev reel of every activity with a heads-up display. Nothing is published yet, so the only way to watch her do nothing for ten hours is to run it yourself. Status: holding position.

<summary><b>THE WALL, SCREEN BY SCREEN</b>: what is real and how it is drawn</summary>

<br>

**Screen 1, the island.** A top-down chart: sand, two depth contours, the palm, the raft, her (the coral blip, pulsing at 80 BPM), a kumara that grows in steps over the run, and a shark in headphones on a slow orbit, nodding on the beat. The turtle, the hydrofoil, the sandcastle and its tide, the coconut and its crab, and the one bar of signal at the top of the palm all happen in the video. Their positions on the chart are guesses: the video is one fixed, painted shot, and nobody ever drew it from above until now.

**Screen 2, the Pacific.** Every gag that arrives from somewhere gets a track: the drone, the bottle that comes straight back, the crate with the cat, the tour boat (selfies, no lift), the iced coffee, the ship, the reply in a different bottle, and the cat floating off again. The island's position is vague on purpose. The coastlines were typed in by hand, a point every few degrees, so the Pacific is roughly where you left it.

**Screen 3, the run.** The clock, the bar counter (ten hours is 12000 bars of 3 seconds) and the four timers' counters all land on the real numbers at 10:00. The board then blanks, the coastlines redraw stroke by stroke, and the loop starts again on a fully drawn board.

**How.** One SVG, built by a plain Node script with no dependencies: no fonts, no filters, no script. Every glowing line exists once and is drawn four times through `<use>` (a wide faint halo, a glow, the line, a thin white-hot core), with the widths passed down as CSS custom properties. The lettering is a single-stroke alphabet defined in the generator. Counters are strips of stroke digits under a clip path; the digits that change faster than a screen can show (minutes, units of bars) spin in phase with the true count, so everything still stops exactly on 10:00 and 12000. With reduced motion switched on, the board holds still on its end-of-run frame, every counter final and every track drawn.

The look is borrowed from the big board of early-1980s hacker cinema, best known from the film *WarGames* (1983), whose wall of rear-projected vector maps was made by Colin Cantwell. It is credited here as the style's origin: none of its names, insignia, signage, dialogue or art is used, and every track on this board is a delivery, a visit or a coffee. Castaway is an unofficial project inspired by *Johnny Castaway* (1992), which belongs to its owners; nothing from it is used here. Low Stakes Command, MOLLUSC and everyone else on the wall are made up.
