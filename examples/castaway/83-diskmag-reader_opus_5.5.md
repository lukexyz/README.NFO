<h1 align="center">Castaway</h1>

<p align="center">
  <b>Issue #00 of the only diskmag printed on one very small island. Ten hours long. Almost nothing happens, on purpose.</b><br>
  <sub>working title &middot; in development, nothing published yet &middot; an unofficial remake inspired by the 1992 screensaver <i>Johnny Castaway</i></sub>
</p>

**Castaway** is a stationary-frame lo-fi video for YouTube, in the spirit of the ten-hour lofi streams: a young woman alone on a tiny island with one tall palm, a raft and a lot of time. She mostly idles, nodding to the music on her headphones, and every so often something happens. A message in a bottle washes straight back. A delivery drone lowers a parcel, and the parcel is another pair of headphones. A coconut falls on a hermit crab, then walks off with the crab wearing it. It is an unofficial remake inspired by the small-island routines and visual comedy of *Johnny Castaway*, the 1992 desert-island screensaver, painted in a sunny, hand-painted coastal anime look: 16:9, 1080p, 30 fps, and always daytime.

More than 90 activities wait on four timers, from every few minutes to once every few hours, and each one starts on the next bar of the music, so every gag lands on the beat. **Every sound is synthesized from code**: no samples, no loops, no recordings. Our reporter sat through the whole default run and filed the party report up there. Nine ships sailed past behind her back. The one she did spot tooted and kept going.

<summary><b>01 EDITORIAL</b>, by Marram</summary>

<br>

Welcome to issue #00 of **CASTAWAY**, the only diskmag printed on one very small island.

This issue covers a ten-hour lo-fi video for YouTube: one young woman, one tall palm, one raft and a lot of time. She mostly idles, nodding to her headphones. Every so often, on the next bar of the music, something happens. The rest of the time is the main feature.

The picture is a sunny, hand-painted coastal anime scene, 16:9, rendered at 1080p and 30 fps. It is always daytime: that is a house rule, and we respect it. Hard cuts and stepped movement are the motion defaults, so the island in the banner moves one pixel at a time, out of respect.

Scene life has 26 entries: shore waves and drifting cloud shadows are built; distant birds, planes with vapour trails, whale pods, dolphins, sailboats, sandpipers, a gecko and a rain shower are planned. The log of every decision so far is in [MUSING.md](MUSING.md).

We sent a reporter. The reporter is still there.

**About that studio.** Every sound is synthesized by [tools/make_audio.py](tools/make_audio.py): no samples, no stock loops, no recordings, so no third-party licence applies. There are more than 150 sound files so far. The theme is a seamless 60-second loop at 80 BPM in F major (ii-V-I-vi), 20 bars of exactly 3 seconds, with electric piano, a kalimba lead, soft drums and vinyl crackle; the ocean ambience is a seamless 60-second loop too. The mix sits at -14 LUFS with true peak at or below -1 dBTP, and levels can be set on the master and per routine. Nobody has listened to any of it yet, so the tune player up there shows a level meter and keeps quiet about the rest. The banner's own loop is 60 seconds, one pass of the theme: its clock counts to 0:59 and every page turn lands on a bar.

<summary><b>05 INVITATION</b>: the way in</summary>

```sh
python tools/serve.py               # the renderer: open http://127.0.0.1:8765/
python tools/schedule.py            # check the schedule and simulate ten hours
python tools/make_audio.py          # every sound, again (numpy + ffmpeg)
python tools/render_demo.py --dev   # a dev reel of every activity, with a HUD
```

The page at <b>http://127.0.0.1:8765/</b> ([web/index.html](web/index.html)) is the renderer: press Play for the live preview, or export a YouTube-ready MP4. It is plain ES modules, with no build step and no npm packages. It encodes frame-exact H.264 in the browser with WebCodecs (68 to 78 frames a second at 1080p30 in Chrome), and the server mixes the sound and joins the two into an MP4. The default run is 10:00:00 on seed 1992. `tools/render_demo.py` is the older Python reference renderer.

In development: no video has been published yet, so for now the only way to watch her wait is to run it.
