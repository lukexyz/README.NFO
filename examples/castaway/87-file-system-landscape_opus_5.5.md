<h3 align="center">CASTAWAY <sub><i>(working title)</i></sub></h3>

<p align="center">
  <b>A ten-hour lo-fi island video, seen from the air as the folders it is made of.</b><br>
  Every box up there is a real file. She is not a file. She is idling.
</p>

<p align="center">
  <kbd>ROOT</kbd> 1 island &nbsp;·&nbsp; <kbd>PALMS</kbd> 1 &nbsp;·&nbsp; <kbd>RUN</kbd> 10:00:00 &nbsp;·&nbsp; <kbd>SEED</kbd> 1992 &nbsp;·&nbsp; <kbd>CAMERA</kbd> fixed (in the video)
</p>

Castaway is a stationary-frame lo-fi video for YouTube, in the spirit of the ten-hour lofi streams: a young woman alone on a tiny island with one tall palm, a raft and a lot of time. She mostly idles, nodding to the music in her headphones, and every so often something happens. A message in a bottle washes straight back. A drone delivers a parcel, and the parcel is another pair of headphones. A shark in headphones nods along. It is an unofficial remake inspired by the small-island routines and visual comedy of *Johnny Castaway*, the 1992 desert-island screensaver, in a sunny, hand-painted coastal anime look: 16:9, 1080p, 30 fps, and always daytime.

[activities.toml](activities.toml) puts more than 90 activities on four timers, from something every few minutes to something every few hours, and lanes let them overlap. She is busy about a third of the time. Every activity waits for the next bar of the music, every 3 seconds, so the gags land on the beat. Every sound is synthesized from code by [tools/make_audio.py](tools/make_audio.py): no samples, loops or recordings.

```sh
python tools/serve.py
# then open http://127.0.0.1:8765/
```

Live preview in the page, then export a YouTube-ready MP4: the browser encodes frame-exact H.264 (WebCodecs) and the server mixes in the sound. Plain ES modules, no build step, no npm. In development: nothing is published yet, so for now the only fly-through is the one above.

[tools/schedule.py](tools/schedule.py) checks the timers; `python tools/render_demo.py --dev` ([tools/render_demo.py](tools/render_demo.py)) renders a dev reel of every activity with a heads-up display.

<details>
<summary><b>How to read the landscape</b></summary>
<br>

- **Box: a file.** Its height is its size in bytes on a log scale, so a 3 KB file is a step and a 10 MB one is a tower block. Its colour is the time since it was last written, at the moment of the snapshot: coral under 6 hours, yellow under a day, green older. Nothing on the island was older than two days.
- **Pedestal: a folder.** Its height is the total size of everything in it, also on a log scale, and its name is on the front. The root folder is the island, which is why it has sand on top.
- **Wire: parent to child.** The pulses do not mean anything. They never did, in these things.
- **Glass tower: a code folder** (`web/js` and `web/js/routines`), written all over with its real file names. The names on the far faces read backwards through the glass, and a highlight bar steps down each list, one line per beat.
- **Striped box: the rest of a very big folder**, stacked into one box so the picture stays light. Folders below the third level fold into their parent; `renders/`, `tmp/` and caches are left out.
- **Snapshot: 2026-10-01**, read-only. The generator keeps its own copy of the sizes and ages, so the picture does not change when the project does. Rebuild it with `node src/87-file-system-landscape_opus_5.5.mjs`.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>The schedule</b>: what happens, and how rarely</summary>
<br>

| Timer | Comes round every | In a typical 10-hour run |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| regular | 2 to 5 minutes | about 155 |
| occasional | 12 to 25 minutes | about 30 |
| rare | 30 to 60 minutes | about 13 |
| super rare | 3 to 6 hours (at most 3 a run) | about 2 |

Typical counts are the median of 200 simulated runs, plus chained follow-ups that only start when something else asks for them (the tide comes back for the sandcastle). Lanes let things overlap: she has one, and the cat, the turtle, the sea and sky, and the shore each have their own. The default run is 10:00:00 with seed 1992.

A taste: the bottle that washes straight back, and the different bottle, much later, that brings a reply. The drone and its parcel of headphones. A sea turtle who visits. A grey tabby with a white chest who arrives on a crate, climbs the palm, naps, and one day floats away again (and comes back another time). The signal hunt: one bar of signal, at the top of the palm. A shark in headphones nodding to the beat. A tour boat of selfie-takers. A coconut that falls on a hermit crab, after which the coconut walks off with the crab wearing it. A bro on an electric hydrofoil who throws a shaka and carves off. Fire by friction, a hammock, a lookout up the palm, spear fishing, and a kumara she plants that grows over the course of the video. And "she could leave any time": she walks out over the water and comes back with an iced coffee.

<details>
<summary><b>The sound</b>: all of it made from code</summary>
<br>

[tools/make_audio.py](tools/make_audio.py) synthesizes everything: no samples, loops or recordings, so no third-party licence applies. More than 150 sound files so far. The theme is a seamless 60-second loop at 80 BPM in F major (ii-V-I-vi), 20 bars of exactly 3 seconds, with electric piano, a kalimba lead, soft drums and vinyl crackle; the ocean ambience is a seamless 60-second loop too. The mix sits at -14 LUFS with true peak at or below -1 dBTP, and levels are adjustable in master and per routine. Nobody has listened to it yet. It is in `media/audio/`, which on the map is the pedestal whose boxes are all coral: every sound file was under six hours old.

</details>
