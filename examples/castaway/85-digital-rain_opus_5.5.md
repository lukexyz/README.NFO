<h1 align="center">Castaway</h1>

<p align="center">
  <b>A ten-hour lo-fi island video in which almost nothing happens, on purpose.</b><br>
  She idles. Every so often, something happens. Every sound you hear was written as code.<br>
  <sub>working title · in development, nothing published yet · an unofficial remake inspired by the 1992 screensaver <i>Johnny Castaway</i></sub>
</p>

**Castaway** is a stationary-frame lo-fi video for YouTube, in the spirit of the ten-hour lofi streams: one tiny island, one tall palm, one raft, and a young woman in cream headphones who is in no hurry at all. Mostly she idles and nods along. Then, every so often, the schedule drops something on her. A message in a bottle washes straight back. A delivery drone brings a parcel, and the parcel is another pair of headphones. A coconut lands on a hermit crab, and the crab leaves wearing it. It looks sunny and hand-painted, in a coastal anime style, 16:9 at 1080p and 30 fps, and it is always daytime. The rain above is the only rain built so far. It is made of glyphs.

More than 90 activities in [activities.toml](activities.toml) sit on four timers, from everyday routines every 2 to 5 minutes to set pieces every 3 to 6 hours, and every one of them waits for the next bar of the music (3 seconds) before it starts, so the gags land on the beat. Every sound is synthesized from code by [tools/make_audio.py](tools/make_audio.py): no samples, no loops, no recordings, so no third-party licence applies.

```sh
python tools/serve.py
# then open http://127.0.0.1:8765/
```

<sub>Live preview in the browser, then export a frame-exact, YouTube-ready MP4: the page encodes the picture and the server mixes in the sound. Plain ES modules, no build step, no npm packages.</sub>

<summary><b>READING THE RAIN</b>: after a while you stop seeing code and start seeing coconuts</summary>

About one glyph in four in the banner is something from the island. The rest are angular strokes and mirrored digits, drawn for this banner, so the rain reads as code until you look closely. Under the place where the island will appear, the code is already full of shells, starfish and palms.

**None of the glyphs move.** 75 columns by 18 rows of them are drawn once, in white. Only the light falls: each column has a strip of light (black, a tail ramping up through deep teal and aqua, then one white head cell) that steps down one cell at a time, laid over the glyphs with a multiply blend, so every glyph takes the colour of the light on it. Columns differ in speed, trail length and timing, at 3.5 to 5 cells a second: slow, on purpose, because there are ten hours to fill. Hard cuts and stepped movement are the project's own motion defaults too. A few dozen cells also swap to a different glyph now and then.

**The words are written by the rain.** A cell of the name locks at the exact step a head arrives in it, and unlocks when a later head comes through. The minute goes like this:

| Time | In the banner |
| --- | --- |
| 0:00 | CASTAWAY is already locked on the first frame; a head passing behind a letter makes one of its glyphs flash |
| 0:14 | the rain washes the letters away, head by head |
| 0:20 | the rain writes the island instead: sun, clouds, horizon, palm, sand, raft, and her, nodding on every beat |
| 0:30 | on the bar line, a coconut lands on a hermit crab, which walks off wearing it, one cell per beat (in the real schedule this is an occasional gag) |
| 0:38 | the rain takes the island back, and from 0:44 writes CASTAWAY again |

One loop is 60 seconds, the length of the theme: 20 bars of 3 seconds at 80 BPM. With reduced motion switched on, the banner holds its first frame, with the name lit and the rain stopped mid-fall.

<br>

```text
 ISLAND OUTLOOK, NEXT 10 HOURS                     RUN 10:00:00 · SEED 1992
 ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

                                               turtle, a coconut on a
                                               hermit crab, a kumara
                                               planted in the sand
 RARE         every 30 to 60 min    about 13   the drone, the signal hunt,
                                               the cat on a crate, the
                                               shark in headphones, the
                                               tour boat, fire by friction
 SUPER RARE   every 3 to 6 hours     about 2   she could leave any time
              (at most 3 a run)                (and does, and comes back
                                               with an iced coffee)
 FOLLOW-UPS   chained, not timed               the tide takes the
                                               sandcastle; a different
                                               bottle brings a reply
 ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
 BUSY         about a third of the time
 IDLE         the rest. nodding. on the beat
 SHOWERS      one, planned, in daylight. not built yet
```

Counts are the medians of 200 simulated 10-hour runs, as the top of [activities.toml](activities.toml) says. On 2026-10-01 the file held 94 activities, 81 on the four timers and 13 chained follow-ups, and more arrive every few hours. Each timer picks by weight among the activities that are free right now. Lanes let things overlap: she has one, and the cat, the turtle, the sea and sky, and the shore each have their own, so the sea can be busy while she is. A ship has been known to wait for exactly that.

Also in the forecast: a stray cat (grey tabby, white chest) that arrives on a crate, climbs the palm, naps, and one day floats away again, then comes back another time; the signal hunt, which ends with one bar at the top of the palm; a tour boat of selfie-takers; a bro on an electric hydrofoil who throws a shaka and carves off; bushcraft (fire by friction, a hammock, a lookout up the palm, spear fishing); a kumara that grows over the course of the video; and waving for rescue, at nobody.

The scene has a life of its own: 26 entries, 4 always-on effects and 22 timed events. The shore waves and the drifting cloud shadows are built. Distant birds, planes with vapour trails, whale pods, dolphins, sailboats, sandpipers, a gecko and that one rain shower are planned.

<summary><b>EVERY SOUND IS CODE</b>: the theme, the sea, and the tools</summary>
<br>

```text
 ── THE SOUND ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  source ...... tools/make_audio.py. no samples, no loops, no recordings,
                so no third-party licence applies
  files ....... more than 150, and counting
  theme ....... a seamless 60 s loop at 80 BPM in F major, ii-V-I-vi,
                20 bars of exactly 3 s: electric piano, a kalimba lead,
                soft drums, vinyl crackle
  ocean ....... also a seamless 60 s loop
  levels ...... -14 LUFS, true peak at or below -1 dBTP, adjustable for
                the master and for each routine
  heard by .... nobody, yet. it has been measured, not listened to

 ── THE TOOLS ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  python tools/serve.py ............. the renderer: a web page with live
                                      preview at http://127.0.0.1:8765/
                                      and export to a YouTube-ready MP4
  python tools/schedule.py .......... validate the schedule and simulate
                                      a 10-hour run
  python tools/render_demo.py --dev . a dev reel of every activity with a
                                      heads-up display (the older Python
                                      reference renderer)
```

The export is frame-exact H.264, encoded in the browser with WebCodecs at 68 to 78 frames a second for 1080p30 in Chrome; the server mixes the sound and joins the two into one MP4. The renderer page itself is [web/index.html](web/index.html), started by [tools/serve.py](tools/serve.py). [tools/schedule.py](tools/schedule.py) checks the schedule, [tools/render_demo.py](tools/render_demo.py) is the reference renderer, and the working notes live in [MUSING.md](MUSING.md).
